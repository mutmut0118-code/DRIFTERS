# Making shops worth more than looting

Design draft, 2026-11-12. **Not approved yet: nothing here is built.** The user's words are quoted; everything marked
**(my call)** is a proposal to say yes or no to. Written while the user was away, so the three questions at the end
are the ones I would have asked one at a time.

## Why

The user, approving the coin economy: "when it comes to trading with a shopkeeper its kinda nonspecial. i need to find
a reason to find the stuff in the shop truly valuable. i can get iron armor just by killing a random drifter than
paying for it. what are some ways that the trading system and economy can be more valuable to the player than other
methods of attaining an item?"

Of the ways offered, the user picked three ("Yes, those three"):

1. **New against battered.** What a shop sells is better than what falls off a body.
2. **Blood goods are recognised.** A dead townsperson's kit is known for what it is.
3. **Things only money buys.** Some things can't be looted at all.

Left for later (not picked): prices that differ from region to region, and a keeper's "back shelf" for people it
trusts.

## What exists today (so the proposals fit it)

- A Drifter drops everything it carries when it dies, as a player does. Its gear is worn by however much it used it;
  kits made for soldiers and for Drifters caught up after time away start 10 to 60 percent worn.
- A town's **stall** sells what the town has plenty of and buys what it's short of; it doesn't deal in gear.
- A town's **smith, fletcher, fisher and enchanter** make what they sell and sell it at their own doors in the
  morning. The smith also **repairs**, the enchanter **enchants**, and a smith can fit **netherite**, each for a fee.
- A town's people wear its **uniform trim** on their armour.
- Fines are paid to a guard; a bounty on the player is paid off nowhere.

## 1. New against battered

**The idea:** looting gets you used gear; a shop gets you gear worth owning.

- **A smith's goods are new and sound:** full durability, always. (Already true: they're freshly made. Said here so it
  stays true.)
- **A smith's goods are sometimes better than plain (my call):** a smith who has been at it a long time now and then
  turns out a piece with a small enchantment already on it (Unbreaking, Protection, Sharpness at its first levels),
  priced higher. It makes them the way a player would: at an enchanting table or with a book, using its own levels and
  lapis. Nothing appears from nowhere.
- **Looted gear is as worn as it really is (my call: no new rule).** I looked at whether a dead Drifter's gear should
  take extra damage when it drops. I recommend against it: a player's gear doesn't break when they die, and the
  Drifter is meant to follow player rules. What I'd do instead is make sure every Drifter's gear carries honest wear:
  today some kits are handed out nearly new, and those should start as worn as their owner's history says (a guard of
  two years has patched armour; a newcomer has what it came with).
- **Repair is the cheap way to keep good gear good (my call):** the smith's repair fee drops well below the price of a
  new piece, so "buy once, pay to keep it" beats "kill someone every time it wears out".
- **The item says where it came from (my call):** a bought piece carries a line under its name, "Made by Ansel of
  Hollowbrook". Purely for the feel of owning it; it does nothing else.

## 2. Blood goods are recognised

**The idea:** you can take a dead townsperson's armour, but you can't walk about in it or sell it as if nothing
happened.

- **What is recognised:** armour carrying a town's uniform trim that came off one of that town's people who was
  killed (by anyone). The piece is quietly marked when it drops. Armour with no trim, and anything a wanderer or a
  bandit carried, is nobody's to recognise.
- **Only by being seen (fairness):** nothing happens unless one of that town's people actually sees the player wearing
  or holding it. Nobody knows from afar.
- **What its town does (my call, mild first):**
  - Its keeper, smith and stall **won't buy it**, and say why ("i know whose that was").
  - Its people are **wary** of a player seen wearing it: the same drop in standing as a small offence, once per piece.
  - A guard who sees it **asks for it back**: hand it over (throw it to the guard) and that's the end of it; refuse and
    it's a fine, as theft is now.
  - If the player is already known there as that person's killer, wearing their kit is an insult on top: the standing
    drop is doubled.
- **Other towns of the same nation** recognise the trim as their nation's but not whose it was: they won't buy it, and
  that's all.
- **Towns at war with that nation don't mind at all,** and their keepers buy it at the usual price. Enemy kit is a
  trophy there. This gives war loot a real market and peace loot none.
- **Getting rid of the mark (my call):** a smith of a town that doesn't care (an enemy's, or a far-off nation's) will
  re-trim the piece in its own colours for a fee, and then it's just armour. There's no way to do it for free.
- **Drifters are held to it as well:** a Drifter that loots a townsperson's trimmed armour is treated the same by
  that town's guards. (Bandits already aren't welcome, so in practice this only matters for townsfolk looting their
  neighbours.)

## 3. Things only money buys

**The idea:** some of the most useful things in the world aren't items at all, so they can't be looted.

Proposed, in the order I'd build them:

- **Made to order (my call: first).** Tell a smith what you want (a tier it can make, and which piece), pay half
  now, and it's ready in a day or two: new, and with the town's trim or none, as you like. The smith needs the
  materials: it uses its own stock, or you bring the ingots and pay less. This is the only way to get exactly the
  piece you want without making it yourself.
- **A hired sword (my call: second).** Pay a town's guard captain (or a willing fighter) by the day and one of its
  people follows and fights for you, in their own gear, for as long as you pay. They go home if unpaid, if you turn on
  them, or if you take them to war against their own nation. Drifter already has "come with me" for friends; this is
  the paid version, for strangers.
- **Buying off a price on your head (my call: third).** The leader of the town that set the bounty will lift it for a
  large sum, to the town's till, once the fine is also paid. Killing your way out stays possible and stays expensive
  in standing.
- **Later, each on your word:** a bed for the night at a town's inn (somewhere safe to sleep and set your spawn, a few
  bronze); passage with a trade caravan; a plot and a house in a town.

With Numismatic Overhaul these are all priced in coins on the scale already agreed (an emerald's worth 1,000 bronze):
a made-to-order iron chestplate a few gold, a hired sword some tens of silver a day, a bounty several gold.

## Goals

- A player who shops ends up better equipped than one who loots, without looting being forbidden or pointless.
- Killing townsfolk for their kit has a cost that is felt where it was done, and nowhere it couldn't be known.
- Money has uses beyond items.

## Anti-goals (it's a failure if)

- Gear is damaged, removed or refused by a rule the player couldn't have foreseen or seen happen.
- A town reacts to something none of its people saw.
- A smith's better goods, or a hired sword's kit, appear from nowhere.
- Looting bandits, monsters' victims and the enemy in a war is punished.

## Three questions

1. **Worn gear.** I recommend no extra damage when a Drifter dies, only honest wear from its history, better goods
   at the smith and cheap repairs. Is that enough of a gap for you, or do you want looted gear to be clearly worse
   (for example: armour that took the killing blows loses a further share of its durability)?
2. **How hard a town comes down on blood goods.** I've proposed the mild version: won't buy, wary, a guard asks for
   it back. Do you want it harsher (wearing a dead townsperson's kit is itself a crime, wanted on sight)?
3. **Which money-only thing first.** I'd build made-to-order gear, then the hired sword, then buying off a bounty.
   Is that the order you want?
